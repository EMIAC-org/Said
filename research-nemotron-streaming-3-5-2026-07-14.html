What NVIDIA actually ships

Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming is a June 2026 multilingual streaming model. Its cache-aware encoder retains context from prior chunks, rather than repeatedly transcribing overlapping buffers. The official model card supports automatic language detection, punctuation/capitalization, and emits a detected language tag that an app must strip before insertion.

Hindi (hi-IN) is in the model’s 19 “transcription-ready” locales, alongside English, Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese and European languages. That makes it categorically more relevant than the Parakeet model you showed earlier.

Important gap: NVIDIA does not claim Hinglish or Roman-Hinglish accuracy. “Hindi supported” does not prove it will correctly handle mixed sentences such as “bhai PR merge kar do, then deploy karna,” nor that it returns the Roman form AirNote needs.

Features that could materially help AirNote

  • Real partial transcription while the user speaks. This could eventually make dictation feel immediate, rather than waiting after Caps Lock release.
  • Auto language ID. We presently force Hinglish through Whisper’s Hindi mode. Nemotron can choose among its supported locales, which may reduce bad manual routing for pure English/Hindi.
  • One latency knob. We could benchmark 320–560 ms for accuracy and 80–160 ms for live captions using the same checkpoint.
  • Native punctuation and casing. Helpful before polish, though this is not a substitute for AirNote’s personal-context polish layer.
  • A credible Hindi benchmark. NVIDIA reports 6.81 WER on FLEURS Hindi at a 1.12 s chunk with language ID. This cannot be compared to Oriserve until both run on the same AirNote clips.

What would make adoption expensive or risky

  • It is not Whisper-compatible. Our current local STT path is hard-wired to Whisper/GGML; this needs a second engine and provider contract.
  • The screenshot’s 716 MB Q8_0 is about 4.8× our 148 MB download. Even a community Q4 GGUF is about 458 MB; that is not NVIDIA’s official runtime.
  • NVIDIA officially documents NeMo on Linux/NVIDIA hardware. A Mac and Windows implementation may be possible through an alternate native/ONNX/GGUF runtime, but it must be treated as its own product integration—not assumed from the model card.
  • Streaming partials create a UX problem: partial text can change as more audio arrives. We would need a stable/unstable-token policy before typing into arbitrary focused apps.

Recommended next move

Do a narrow benchmark before any integration: 30–50 real clips split among English, pure Hindi, Roman Hinglish, Hindi-English code switching, names/technical terms, and noisy audio. Score word error, Romanization correctness, term errors, final latency, partial-text stability, model download/RAM, and CPU/GPU behavior.

Pass condition: it must beat—or meaningfully broaden—Oriserve on Hindi/Hinglish without compromising the existing local lane. Until then, keep Oriserve as the default and treat Nemotron as an optional experimental provider.
